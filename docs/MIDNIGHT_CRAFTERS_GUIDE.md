# The Midnight Crafter’s Guide

Retail Midnight 12.1 / Season 2 · research snapshot: 25 September 2026

This is a self-contained guide to modern Retail crafting for someone whose mental model comes from Cataclysm. It is written to explain the system, not to tell you to switch professions or pursue every possible optimisation.

## The short version

Modern crafting is a quality calculation, not “learn recipe, add materials, receive fixed item.” A high-end crafter has built a specialised workshop over time. The live Crafting window's **Expected Quality** bar, with the actual selected inputs, is the trustworthy answer for what that crafter can deliver now.

For a character with Mining and Engineering, it is completely normal to keep those professions and order combat gear from another specialist. Crafting Orders exist so a player does not need to abandon preferred professions merely to obtain bind-on-pickup crafted gear.

## 1. Pick the job you want your profession to do

| Goal | Good plan | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Obtain strong personal gear | Keep enjoyable professions; use negotiated Personal orders for specialist gear. | Changing professions just because a guide calls an item best-in-slot. |
| Make useful Engineering items | Focus on one Engineering family: utility/profession equipment or Combat Analytics/guns. | Spending early Knowledge Points across every attractive node. |
| Gather/casual gold | Mine, sell/use materials, and make only things you understand and enjoy. | Buying a mass-crafting route without checking present demand/cost. |
| High-end order crafter | Focus one recipe family, collect every recurring Knowledge Point source, improve tools, and guarantee only what your UI proves. | Advertising every known recipe as rank 5. |

## 2. Read the crafting screen from top to bottom

| Screen term | What it means |
|---|---|
| Recipe Difficulty | The amount of relevant crafting skill needed for a quality result—the height of the wall. |
| Profession Skill | Your basic 1–100 skill plus bonuses from relevant specialisation and profession equipment. A gun bonus may not help a goggle recipe. |
| Required reagents | Mandatory materials. Their quality can change the result. Buy higher quality only when it crosses a real quality breakpoint. |
| Optional reagents | Customisation such as stat missive, embellishment, Spark, or crest/infusion. These may also raise recipe difficulty. |
| Finishing reagent | A crafter-side, one-use modifier. Read the current tooltip and recipe slot; old-expansion assumptions are unreliable. |
| Expected Quality | Preview result for *this exact* crafter, materials, and optional inputs. This is the crucial bar. |

Example: a customer wants a Spark mail item with Haste/Mastery and an embellishment. The Spark selects its high-end bracket; the missive chooses Haste/Mastery; the embellishment supplies its effect. After all are selected, inspect Expected Quality. If it misses the agreed rank, use better materials, more relevant skill, a permitted finishing reagent, Concentration, or another specialist. Do not merely hope for a proc.

## 3. The five dials of quality

1. **Skill:** the visible profession level. It unlocks recipes and gives a base contribution, but it is not the whole system.
2. **Knowledge Points (KP):** permanent points spent in specialisation trees. They unlock recipe families and provide category-specific skill/profession-stat bonuses. Treat them as a build.
3. **Profession equipment:** crafter-only tool/accessory slots that add profession skill/stats. They are not combat equipment or customer inputs.
4. **Reagent quality:** an input-cost versus output-quality dial. Select it based on the quality bar, rather than reflexively buying maximum rank.
5. **Concentration:** a 0–1,000 reserve per crafting profession per character. It can guarantee the next quality tier when sufficient and regenerates one point per six minutes (10/hour). It is valuable.

Profession stats:

- **Resourcefulness:** chance to use fewer materials.
- **Multicraft:** chance to make extra stackable output. It does not create a second weapon or armour item.
- **Ingenuity:** chance to refund some spent Concentration.
- **Crafting Speed:** time only.

Equipment has five quality ranks. Most Midnight stackable reagents and consumables have two quality ranks. Cooking does not use the same quality/Concentration system.

## 4. Crafter progression: a deliberate first-week plan

1. Learn the **Midnight** version of your profession. Expansion profession skills are separate.
2. Reach skill 25 and open specialisation. Do not treat 100/100 as the only goal.
3. Choose one destination before spending KP.
   - Engineering: profession equipment/utility **or** Combat Analytics/guns.
   - Mining: gathering comfort/fundamentals, then an ore or infused-mechanic focus.
4. Do inexpensive/meaningful **First Crafts** once. They contribute structural progression; do not buy piles of worthless crafts solely for a number.
5. Each reset, collect profession treasures and do the trainer weekly, relevant Treatise, and useful Patron orders.
6. Use profession-specific Artisan's Moxie on recipes/manuals that serve the chosen branch.
7. Improve profession equipment once you have committed to using the profession enough to justify it.

KP is permanent. Some guide pages claim a profession reset is live in 12.1, but Blizzard describes a reset as upcoming 12.1.5 PTR content. Do not rely on a reset until it is actually present in the live profession UI.

## 5. Orders, explained from both sides

| Type | Who can fulfill it | Best use | Important limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public | Any eligible crafter | Cheap consumables; low-risk order-count tasks | Cannot guarantee a requested quality. Do not use for serious gear. |
| Personal | The named crafter | Important gear and recrafts | Requires finding/negotiating with the specialist. |
| Guild | Eligible guild crafters | Trusted service and shared materials | Still verify specialisation and quality. |
| Patron | NPC contract for the crafter | KP/Moxie and profession progression | Not a customer gear service; can be a poor gold deal. |

### Customer checklist for a serious Personal order

- State the exact item and Season 2 Spark/crest infusion.
- State desired secondary stats if a missive is applicable.
- State whether it is embellished and count currently equipped embellishments. You may equip only two embellished items.
- State the minimum accepted quality: say “rank 5,” not “best.”
- Agree who provides normal materials, the commission, and whether the price includes Concentration.
- Ask the crafter to inspect your exact material qualities and confirm the expected result before submitting.

### Crafter checklist before accepting

- Open the order at the proper crafting station.
- Check customer reagent ranks, optional reagents, requested quality, commission, and your Expected Quality.
- State whether the requested quality is guaranteed normally, guaranteed with Concentration, or unavailable.
- Do not silently provide costly materials to rescue an unprofitable order; negotiate first.
- Check item, stats and optional reagents again before completing it.

Public orders are fine for low-risk work. They are the wrong way to commission a high-quality Spark weapon or an embellishment-critical gear piece.

## 6. Concentration: when to spend it

Spend Concentration when it converts a known valuable craft from one quality tier to the next:

- a negotiated customer commission where you promised the result;
- an important personal gear piece;
- a deliberately profitable concentration craft after you understand its margins.

Do not spend it on levelling/vendor crafts, cheap crafts with no meaningful quality difference, or every order merely to appear capable. A focused crafter eventually relies more on specialisation, skill and materials; Concentration is the bridge and insurance.

## 7. Sparks, infusions and recrafting

**Spark of Tides** is a character-bound time gate for Season 2 high-end crafted gear. Most epic pieces use two Sparks; a two-handed weapon uses four. A Spark answers “this character is allowed to make this high-end piece,” not “this is automatically the right item to craft.”

Use a Spark on a durable weak slot, selected embellishment, or weapon after checking current spec priorities. Do not use it merely because it is available.

Later, **recraft** the same item to change:

- secondary-stat missive;
- quality;
- embellishment;
- crest/infusion item-level bracket.

Recrafting does not consume a new Spark for the same item. However, replaced optional reagents are destroyed, and low-quality original materials can still influence the calculation. Arrange the recraft as a Personal order and get a new quality guarantee.

An item can be crafted with an embellishment, but only **two embellished items** may be equipped. Crafting three is possible; equipping all three is not.

## 8. Practical Mining and Engineering path

### Mining

Mining is a gathering/material profession, not a direct source of combat gear.

- **Finesse** improves material quantity.
- **Perception** improves rare finds.
- **Deftness** improves gathering speed.

A comfortable casual path begins with fundamentals/mounted gathering, then a chosen ore or infused-mechanic direction. Gather one-time knowledge sources and recurring weekly drops; avoid spreading KP across every branch early.

### Engineering

Engineering is worthwhile for utility, profession equipment, selected gear/guns and enjoyment. It is not mandatory for Hunter combat gearing because Personal orders can obtain other professions’ gear.

Relevant Engineering directions:

- **Market Mobility:** profession tools/accessories.
- **Combat Analytics:** guns/gear and related crafting capability.
- **Recycling:** components/discovery work.
- **Bits & Bots:** reagents, utility and associated systems.

Choose Market Mobility if self/alt profession support is the goal. Choose Combat Analytics if guns/gear are the goal. Do not blindly recycle expensive inputs: understand the resulting component/discovery/profit first.

### Cooking and Fishing

They are secondary professions with no KP specialisation tree. Cooking supplies food/feasts; Fishing supplies food materials and current-zone ingredients. Both are enjoyable self-sufficiency systems, not character-ilvl progression requirements.

## 9. Common costly mistakes

- Spending KP broadly before choosing a product family.
- Assuming a recipe known equals a rank-5 recipe mastered.
- Using a Public order for quality-critical gear.
- Spending Concentration on disposable crafts.
- Crafting a Spark filler while a better durable slot or embellishment decision is pending.
- Forgetting the two-equipped-embellishment cap.
- Treating Mining and Engineering as a complete material supply chain.
- Buying expensive tools/materials for one craft when a specialist Personal order costs less.
- Believing a presumed future reset will repair an unconsidered KP plan.
